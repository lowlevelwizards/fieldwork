# 2.0L — Silent Withdrawal & De-escalation

## Why this slice exists

2.0K ended with a real outward interaction: Northline issued a mission-specific warning and the Commune heard it. That created the next causal question:

> Does the warned team reply, remain, escalate, or leave—and what can the warning team legitimately infer from the result?

2.0L chooses one complete nonviolent outcome. The Commune withdraws without replying. Northline observes the departure and lets the encounter end.

## Causal chain

```text
warning heard
→ concealed watch judged compromised
→ Withdraw Silently selected
→ Break Contact Quietly procedure
→ staged temporary responsibilities
→ actor movement proposals
→ scheduler and locomotion execution
→ observed withdrawing / lost activity
→ Monitor Departure selected
→ Boundary Restored
→ consequence memory
```

## Reuse before abstraction

The slice deliberately reuses existing systems:

- The warning is existing heard-communication evidence.
- The team decision ledger selects a response.
- The procedure system owns phases and temporary roles.
- Actors derive local actions from their current role and phase.
- The scheduler owns lifecycle and locomotion-channel exclusivity.
- Existing destination claims preserve spacing.
- The existing locomotion executor moves actors.
- Existing visual contact tracks classify withdrawal and lost contact.
- Existing encounter reassessment lets Northline de-escalate.

Only two focused runtime concepts are added:

- `WithdrawToRoute` — one actor’s procedure-authorized movement action.
- Encounter outcome memory — the durable consequence after both teams have evidence that the encounter ended.

There is no second squad-movement engine and no general negotiation framework.

## Authored withdrawal plan

The Commune mission defines one route plan:

- A shared exit area.
- A distinct destination offset for each temporary role.
- Movement speed and arrival tolerance.
- Minimum destination spacing.

The mission supplies permitted geography. It does not start movement or determine who moves first.

## Break Contact Quietly

```text
Establish Responsibilities
→ Lead Withdrawal
→ Protected Movement
→ Rear Disengage
→ Withdrawal Complete
```

Temporary roles:

- **Withdrawal Lead** — moves first and establishes the route.
- **Protected Mover** — follows after the lead reaches the route.
- **Rear Watch** — maintains contact awareness and leaves last.

A completed movement action emits a procedure event. The procedure advances one phase. Actors in inactive stages hold or observe rather than independently beginning movement.

## Observable de-escalation

Northline never reads the Commune response, procedure, roles, or route reason. It can observe only consequences:

- A contact begins moving away.
- Additional contacts follow.
- The group leaves the monitored area.
- No reply or hostile act occurs.

That evidence makes **Monitor Departure** preferable to warning again, pursuing, or escalating.

## Outcome memory

When the withdrawal is complete and Northline has actually observed departure after its warning, each team stores a different outcome:

### Commune

```text
Withdrew without violence
Warning heard
No reply sent
Team withdrew
No hostile act observed
```

### Northline

```text
Contact departed after warning
Warning issued
Departure observed
No reply heard
No hostile act observed
```

These are evidence-grounded memories, not a global reputation score or omniscient shared event record.

## Explicit exclusions

2.0L does not add:

- Spoken compliance, refusal, deception, or negotiation trees.
- Automatic hostility from silence.
- Pursuit or interception.
- Combat cover selection.
- Target selection, aiming, suppression, or firing.
- General faction reputation.
- Continuous route optimization or destination churn.

The slice ends when the teams recognize that the encounter concluded without violence.
