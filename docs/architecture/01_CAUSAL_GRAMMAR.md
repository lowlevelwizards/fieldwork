# Fieldwork Causal Grammar

The architecture grows from causes rather than genre conventions. Every layer must justify itself by answering a question created by the layer above it.

```text
FACT
→ VALUE
→ NEED
→ GOAL
→ OPERATION
→ MISSION
→ TASK
→ KNOWLEDGE
→ ENCOUNTER
→ RESPONSE
→ PROCEDURE
→ ROLE
→ ACTION
→ EXECUTION
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ MEMORY
→ NEW FACT
```

## Fact

Something true about a place, route, resource, structure, person, team, or relationship.

Created by world initialization or consequences.

Produces material that values can interpret.

Must not contain a desired solution.

## Value

Why a faction, team, or person cares about a category of facts.

Created by identity, doctrine, relationships, and commitments.

Produces significance.

Must not directly create missions or actions.

## Need

A meaningful discrepancy between a valued desired condition and believed current reality.

Created when knowledge of a fact is interpreted through values.

Produces pressure to define a change.

Must not prescribe a method.

## Goal

A testable desired world-state change.

Created from a recognized need.

Produces success criteria.

Must describe the result, not the steps.

## Operation

A persistent organized attempt to achieve a goal.

Created when a faction selects a method and commits resources.

Produces phases, resource requirements, assigned teams, risk constraints, and fallback plans.

Must not control individual movement or weapon use.

## Mission

A team’s bounded responsibility within an operation.

Created when an operation assigns embodied participants.

Produces an objective, route, required items, time constraints, risk tolerance, success conditions, abort conditions, and return or extraction destination.

Must not prescribe every actor action.

## Task

The team’s immediate objective.

Created by the current mission or operation phase.

Produces a concrete question the team can act upon.

Must remain broad enough for actors to retain autonomy.

## Knowledge

What an actor or team believes about the world, including uncertainty and source.

Created by perception, communication, inference, and memory.

Produces the informational basis for decisions.

Must not silently read omniscient world state.

## Encounter

A situation in which another goal, actor, group, or hazard may interfere with a task or mission.

Created when believed conditions make two intentions or requirements incompatible.

Produces an obstruction, escalation state, and available response space.

Must not assume violence is required.

## Response

The team’s chosen approach to an encounter or obstruction.

Created by comparing mission value, preservation, disruption, position, certainty, cost, time, and exit options through doctrine.

Produces a tactical intention such as avoid, observe, negotiate, conceal, delay, defend, ambush, disrupt, assault, recover, or withdraw.

Must not directly move actors.

## Procedure

A coordinated, phased method for carrying out a response.

Created when a response requires multiple responsibilities or ordered stages.

Produces phases, roles, permissions, constraints, and completion or failure events.

Must not perform mechanical execution.

## Role

A temporary responsibility required by a procedure.

Created because a procedure needs someone to observe, carry, secure, support, maneuver, treat, document, repair, or withdraw.

Produces actor-level responsibilities and permissions.

Must not become a permanent character class unless another system explicitly requires it.

## Action

A persistent thing an individual is doing.

Created by the actor brain from personal knowledge, assigned responsibility, danger, body, equipment, terrain, opportunities, and permissions.

Produces channel occupation, reservations, progress, completion, failure, interruption, and diagnostic reasons.

Must not directly alter physical state outside an executor.

## Execution

The physical performance of an accepted action.

Created when the scheduler starts an action.

Produces movement, firing, communication, treatment, carrying, repair, or interaction results.

Executors do not choose why an action should occur.

## Consequence

A resulting change to actors, resources, knowledge, relationships, infrastructure, routes, influence, or world conditions.

Created by execution and world rules.

Produces reassessment and new facts.

Must not be discarded when it should affect later play.

## Memory

A retained record that changes later belief or decision-making.

Created when consequences are observed, reported, inferred, or institutionally recorded.

Produces persistent actor, team, faction, and world history.

Must preserve uncertainty and source where relevant.
